A bridge between mathematics and statistical inference

Connect the mathematical objects to their statistical meaning

You may recognize projections and derivatives before you recognize an estimand, or recognize regression and confidence intervals before you recognize a Hilbert space. Both are useful starting points. This reference connects the language; read the substantive pages first when the terminology is new. It is not a gate requiring all later theory before beginning the guide.

The same concepts can be familiar in one language and unfamiliar in the other. The examples below connect the algebra, population quantities, and uncertainty arguments.

One example connects the languages

Suppose Y_1,\ldots,Y_n are independent observations from the same population, E[Y]=\mu, and 0<\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\sigma^2<\infty.

The estimand is \mu: a number describing the population. The estimator is \bar Y: a function of a random sample. The estimate is its numerical value for the sample you collected. Repeating the sampling would usually change the estimate while leaving the population target fixed.

The estimating equation P_n(Y-a)=0, where a is a candidate mean and P_n means a sample average, has solution \hat\mu=\bar Y. Evaluating its error around the population mean gives the exact identity

\hat\mu-\mu=P_n(Y-\mu).

The centered contribution Y-\mu has expectation zero and variance \sigma^2. It will also be the influence function for the population mean in the unrestricted model. For now, follow the three statistical steps supplied by this identity.

First, the LLN makes the average contribution approach zero in probability, so \hat\mu is consistent for \mu. Second, the CLT gives a distribution for its fluctuations:

\sqrt n(\hat\mu-\mu)\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\sigma^2).

The limiting variance \sigma^2 belongs to the scaled error. The estimator itself has variance \sigma^2/n and SE \sigma/\sqrt n. Thus observation variability and mean-estimation uncertainty use the same population variance but different scales.

Third, \sigma^2 is unknown, so we estimate the SE by s_Y/\sqrt n. Variance consistency and Slutsky justify the standardized error

\frac{\hat\mu-\mu}{s_Y/\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).

A first-order algebraic identity, a population-limit argument, and a feasible uncertainty calculation are connected pieces. Each contributes something the others do not establish alone. Large-sample reasoning for methods research walks through the theorem statements and substitutions; Uncertainty, confidence intervals, and tests translates the standardized limit into a confidence procedure. Clustered observations, infinite variance, or a different target require rechecking the argument.

Transfer question: if your target is \sqrt\mu for positive Y, how does the influence contribution change? Work through Checkpoint SP-01.

Functions as vectors

For square-integrable functions, \langle f,g\rangle=E[fg] and \|f\|_2=\sqrt{E[f^2]}. Expected products play the role of dot products. “Square-integrable” means the expected square is finite, so this length is defined.

For the centered measurement f=Y-\mu, the squared length is E[(Y-\mu)^2]=\sigma^2. Its inner product with the constant function 1 is zero because E[Y-\mu]=0. This is the geometric statement that the residual after subtracting the population mean is orthogonal to constants.

The same point follows from an optimization calculation. For a fixed candidate constant c,

E[(Y-c)^2]=\sigma^2+(\mu-c)^2.

Expand Y-c=(Y-\mu)+(\mu-c); the cross term vanishes after taking expectation. The best constant squared-error prediction is therefore \mu. Replacing population expectations by sample averages makes the best constant the sample mean. Conditional expectation and L2 geometry extends this idea from constants to functions of covariates.

Derivatives with respect to a distribution

A target can change when the data-generating distribution changes. For the mean, mix the population law P with a small mass \varepsilon at a value y. The new law P_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\delta_y has mean

\mu(P_\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon y.

Its derivative at zero is y-\mu: the same centered contribution that appears in the sample-mean expansion. This connects a mathematical sensitivity calculation to the statistical variability of an estimator. One calculation changes a population distribution; the other studies a random sample from a fixed population. Establishing that they agree for a general target takes additional work.

Statistical models, targets, and paths explains which paths stay inside a specified model and introduces their scores. Influence functions develops the derivative-to-estimator connection with its assumptions. These concrete examples precede general representation-theorem language.

Read a large-sample statement

  • X_n\to_p x means that the probability of a fixed-size departure from x tends to zero. It does not say X_n=x in a finite sample.
  • X_n\to_d X describes convergence of distributions; it is the kind of approximation used in a CLT.
  • X_n\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}x means convergence along almost every sample path. It is stronger than convergence in probability; our inference arguments use the weaker probability limits. The detailed comparison is in Large-sample reasoning for methods research.
  • \|\hat f-f\|_2\to_p0 describes consistency of an estimated function in population root-mean-square error, holding the fitted function fixed when taking the norm. It is not the same as a small training error.
  • X_n=O_p(a_n) means X_n/a_n is bounded in probability. X_n=o_p(a_n) means that ratio tends to zero in probability. Thus O_p(1) does not imply convergence to zero.

A typical inference argument establishes

\begin{aligned} \hat\theta-\theta_0&=(P_n-P)\phi_0+R_n,\\ R_n&=o_p(n^{-1/2}), \end{aligned}

where P\phi_0=0 and 0<P\phi_0^2<\infty. Scaling by \sqrt n makes the remainder o_p(1); a CLT handles the first term, and Slutsky’s theorem allows a consistent estimated variance in the final standardized statistic.

The rate notation locates an error relative to the scale of the inference. A term n^{-1/2} becomes 1 after root-n scaling and can affect the limit. A term n^{-1} becomes n^{-1/2} and disappears. In a proof, o_p(n^{-1/2}) makes that latter comparison for a random remainder. The probability definitions and product rules are developed in Large-sample reasoning for methods research.

You must show why the remainder is negligible. When \phi is learned from the same observations, treating it as a fixed function is generally unjustified. Cross-fitting helps separate estimation and evaluation, while population bias can remain. The From an influence function to an estimator page derives the decomposition and proves the fold-wise conditional bound (SP-12) before completing inference for the missing-outcome mean (SP-13).

Finite moments and permitted interchanges of derivatives and integrals are assumptions to verify. For bounded-score paths and elementary integrable targets, the calculations can often be checked directly. More general arguments need an appropriate domination or differentiability result.
